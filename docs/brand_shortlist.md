# Phase 1 — Candidate Brand Shortlist

Generated by `scripts/inspect_dataset.py` against the full raw dataset
(`data/raw/customer-support-on-twitter/twcs.csv`, 2,811,774 tweets, 108
distinct brand/support accounts, 1,537,843 customer tweets, 1,273,931
brand tweets, date range 2008-05-08 to 2017-12-03 — though 97% of volume is
concentrated in Sep–Dec 2017). Full numbers: `data/processed/brand_statistics.csv`,
`data/processed/dataset_summary.json`. Example threads for every shortlisted
brand: `data/processed/sample_conversations.txt`.

Definitions used in the table below (see `scripts/inspect_dataset.py` for
exact computation):
- **brand_messages** — tweets authored by the brand's own account.
- **conversations** — of those, how many are direct replies (`in_response_to_tweet_id`
  not null), i.e. actual 1:1 support interactions rather than broadcast tweets.
- **customer_messages** — customer tweets that @-mention the brand handle.
- **reply_ratio** — `conversations / brand_messages`. Near 1.0 means the
  account is almost entirely a dedicated support channel, not general marketing.

All shortlisted brands have a reply_ratio above 0.99 — every one of these
accounts is used almost exclusively for 1:1 support replies rather than
broadcast/marketing, which is a good general sign of data quality across
the dataset (the accounts curated into this dataset are genuinely support
handles, not general brand accounts).

## 5 candidates carried forward, with evidence

### 1. AmazonHelp (e-commerce)
- Largest volume by a wide margin: 169k+ support conversations available.
- Sample threads (`sample_conversations.txt`) show substantive, specific
  resolutions handled *in the public thread* rather than deflected
  entirely to DM — e.g. tweet 1190175: multi-turn thread about a failed
  delivery, working through contacting the courier, ending with a concrete
  next step. Tweet 848236: order-cancellation thread with a clear
  back-and-forth and an explicit outcome.
- Risk: at this volume, problem types will be extremely diverse (orders,
  payments, Prime, devices, pricing) — may need more intents than a
  smaller/narrower brand, or coarser intent buckets.

### 2. Delta (airline)
- Strong volume (42k conversations) with clearly bounded, recurring
  problem types: baggage (tweet 552812/1200088 threads — lost item,
  baggage claim), flight delays, compliments to staff.
- Resolutions are concrete and often policy-based (contact numbers, report
  links, agent follow-up), which is useful for "grounded" reply generation
  since the same handful of resolution patterns recur.
- Risk: multi-turn threads sometimes reference account/flight specifics
  (confirmation numbers) that must be treated as opaque tokens, not
  factual claims the model can echo.

### 3. Tesco (retail/grocery)
- Good volume (38.5k conversations), unusually rich and specific complaint
  text (tweet 2357120: an actual escalation about staff conduct during an
  age-verification (Think25) check, tweet 608599: product
  discontinuation complaint) — these are substantive, human-written
  complaints rather than templated troubleshooting.
- Its "Think25" alcohol-age-verification policy recurs often enough across
  samples that it looks like a natural candidate intent with a consistent,
  explainable historical resolution.
- Risk: UK-specific retail context (delivery slots, in-store policy) may
  be less immediately familiar than a US-centric brand, though this is a
  minor concern for an offline prototype.

### 4. AppleSupport (consumer tech)
- Very large volume (106.7k conversations), highly consistent phrasing
  ("We'd like to help", "Send us a DM") — clean, low-noise account.
- Risk: sample threads show most resolutions are a single templated
  "please DM us" turn with no visible resolution content (device
  troubleshooting genuinely happens in DM, which isn't in this dataset).
  This is a real limitation: for many intents, the "historical response"
  we can ground on is only ever the DM hand-off, not the fix itself.

### 5. SpotifyCares (subscription tech)
- Mid-size volume (43.2k conversations) but the richest *public*
  troubleshooting flows seen in any sample: tweet 2597260 and the
  Oct 11–12 playback-issue thread walk through device/OS/version
  questions, a restart step, and a follow-up when the issue recurs — a
  multi-turn diagnostic conversation that stays in-thread rather than
  immediately deflecting to DM.
- Smaller than the others, which may limit how many cases are available
  per intent once split into knowledge/golden sets.

## Also considered, not shortlisted further

- **Uber_Support** — good volume but sample threads are almost entirely
  one-line "please DM us" deflections with no visible resolution content;
  weakest evidence of in-thread substance among the large-volume brands.
- **AmericanAir** — very similar profile to Delta (same domain); Delta's
  samples showed slightly more resolution detail, so it was picked over
  AmericanAir to avoid carrying two near-duplicate airline candidates
  forward, not because AmericanAir is unusable.

## What Phase 1 established (success criteria)

- **What does the dataset contain?** 2.81M tweets, 108 support accounts,
  customer vs. brand messages cleanly separated by the `inbound` column, no
  missing values in the core fields, 0 duplicate `tweet_id`s, 11
  near-duplicate content rows (negligible).
- **Which brands have enough usable conversations?** All 5 shortlisted
  brands have 38k–169k reply-based conversations — far more than needed for
  a knowledge index + a 150–250-example golden set.
- **Are customer/brand replies identifiable?** Yes — the `inbound` boolean
  plus `author_id` (brand handle vs. anonymized numeric customer id)
  identify the speaker unambiguously.
- **Can conversations be reconstructed?** Yes — `response_tweet_id` /
  `in_response_to_tweet_id` allow walking a thread; `sample_conversations.txt`
  demonstrates 2–5 turn threads reconstructed this way for every
  shortlisted brand. Some threads are truncated where a parent tweet falls
  outside the sampling buffer used for this exploratory pass — full,
  unbounded reconstruction is the job of Phase 3.

Brand selection (Phase 2) will pick one of these five and record the
reasoning in `DECISION_LOG.md`.
